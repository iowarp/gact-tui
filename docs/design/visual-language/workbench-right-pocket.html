Notes
  • Two layers. The strip on the outer edge holds the lists: Files, Sources, Artifacts, Activity and Agent, then Terminal and Browser pages. Tabs hold only what you opened.
  • Click the lit icon to hide the list. The tab you're reading gets the whole width. Clicking any icon brings its list back beside the tabs.
  • The strip is the panel's edge. It sits on the window edge so it never moves when you resize, and its icons line up with everything else that ends a row.
  • A tab knows where it came from. Show in Files reveals it in its list; Add to message puts it in the composer as an @ chip.
  • Teal means the conversation touched it. Changed files and their tabs are teal, the same rule as changed settings. No dots.
  • The rail and the workbench stay separate. The rail floats and glances; the workbench is where you read and work. Rail rows open in Activity.
  • Width is a percentage. 40% to start, then whatever you drag it to, as a share of the window. When the conversation would go under 400px, the left bar folds first; only then does the workbench slide over.
Open questions

  • Where the strip sits. Outer edge (shown) keeps it still. The inner edge, next to the conversation, keeps the lists closer to what mentions them.
